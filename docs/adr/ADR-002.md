# ADR-002: Merchant Organization as the Primary Sovereign Tenant

- **Status**: PROPOSED
- **Deciders**: Lead Orchestrator, System Architect, Product Manager
- **Date**: 2026-11-10
- **Technical Area**: Tenancy & Data Modeling

---

## 1. Context
In the Phase 0 gateway baseline, the data model was customer/buyer-centric. The primary entity was the consumer (`customer_id`, `customer_mandates`, `customer_wallets`, `customer_signing_keys`). The merchant was merely a static filter string (`merchant_id = 'MERCH_ELEC'`) attached to catalog items. The platform's product pivot establishes a **Merchant-First Agentic Commerce Platform**.

## 2. Problem
What entity constitutes the root tenant for data partitioning, security boundaries, employee authorization, AI workforce provisioning, and billing?

## 3. Decision
We decide that the **Merchant Organization (`merchants`) is the primary, authoritative sovereign tenant** of the platform:
- Every store, product, inventory hold, customer order, payment intent, AI agent, and audit log belongs to exactly one `merchant_id`.
- Employees belong to a merchant organization via `merchant_members`.
- Connected stores (WooCommerce, Shopify) belong to a merchant organization.
- Autonomous AI agents are provisioned and governed on a per-merchant basis.
- End-consumer shopper records (`customers`) are scoped within the merchant's customer registry, while retaining global cryptographic mandate verification in the governance module for cross-merchant buyer agents.

## 4. Alternatives Considered
1. **Store-as-Tenant**:
   - *Pros*: Direct mapping to individual e-commerce storefronts.
   - *Cons*: Fails when a merchant operates multiple stores (e.g. India Store + US Store, or WooCommerce + Shopify) sharing the same inventory, staff, and AI agents.
2. **User/Employee-as-Tenant**:
   - *Pros*: Simplistic multi-user accounts.
   - *Cons*: Prevents organization collaboration, role transfer, and corporate ownership of AI workers.

## 5. Consequences
- **Positive**: Enables multi-store management under one business; cleanly partitions AI agent permissions and payment payout configurations; provides clear corporate boundary for GDPR/privacy compliance.
- **Negative**: Adds one foreign key level (`merchant_id`) to all operational tables; requires user-to-tenant switching mechanics in the UI.
- **Neutral**: The legacy Buyer AI gateway flow continues to work by routing to a default demo merchant organization.

## 6. Security Implications
- Every database query mutating or reading business state must be scoped by the merchant context.
- Prevents cross-merchant data leakage (catalog, sales figures, customer PII).

## 7. Migration Implications
- Requires an additive migration adding `merchant_id` to existing tables (`catalog_products`, `audit_records`, `audit_events`, `purchase_approvals`).
- Backfill legacy data into a seed merchant (`MERCH_DEFAULT` / `'MERCH_ELEC'`) before enforcing `NOT NULL` constraints.

## 8. Open Questions
- Should multi-merchant umbrella organizations (enterprise holding companies with multiple sub-merchants) be supported in P1? *Verdict*: Deferred to P2; flat merchant organization is sufficient for P1.
